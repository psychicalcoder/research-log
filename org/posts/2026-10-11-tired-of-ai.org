* From zero AI experience to Frontier AI Agent
As a computer science student who was not interested in AI, I had been
explicitly avoiding AI before June 2026, until I got a summer
internship offer from Roku, a US company making TVs. To be honest, it
was really lucky for me to get the offer without any AI experience
since Roku expected the intern to change the workflow of the company
with AI.

While the world was discussing OpenClaw, I was writing code by
hand. Roku offered me an unlimited budget to use the frontier models
of the time, Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6-sol. The almost abusive use of
AI agents really shocked me when I entered the company. In the
company, the documentation is written by AI and read by AI. AI looks
for the answer from the AI written docs on human's
request. Multiagents automatically monitor and resolve the bugs on bug
trackers. Via project upgrading the BSP kernel from 5.15 to 6.12 for a
model, I realized that on a single task, AI is much better than
me. Since the codebase involves SoC vendors custom drivers, it would
be impossible to complete the kernel upgrade without the support from
the vendor. However, with Claude Opus, all you need is an intern
familiar with embedded systems.

Although the agents are still not reliable enough to do all the
production work today, they can already speed up the work by more than
10 times.

* Article Works
I enjoy predicting the future and trying to answer questions like
"When will the AI bubble collapse?", "Will China take over the US
after the AI bubble collapse?". To make some side product from this
hobby, my friend and I founded a Threads account, [[https://www.threads.com/@navigable.tech][Navigable Tech]] to
talk about the AI industry.

I found that a false argument can sound valid when the proposer does
not disclose the contradictory evidence. We can usually reach
interesting conclusions after eliminating the information
asymmetry. In the era of information explosion, people usually only
consume nth-hand information, such as translated news or even comments
from other people on a forum. Therefore, we aim to provide upper-bound
and lower-bound analysis to reduce the bias of accessible data. This
work requires a huge amount of reading and data mining. We use AI a
lot to systematically collect materials and evidence, so that we don't
need to rely on a single piece of news or an impression to judge a
fact.

* Slop on Social Media
Because of running the Threads Accounts, I started to do some trivial
marketing and read the posts regarding AI. To be honest, the Mandarin
AI community really disgusted me with its ChatGPT slop. Half of the
posts are directly generated by ChatGPT. No one even put effort into
the prompt to reduce the AI scent. It was really a torture to see
these posts.

Recently, the software engineering capability of AI has evolved to the
next level. These AI labs keep polishing the "one-shot" capability.
Given a hard topic and one starting prompt, the agent can build
something matching the goal without any human intervention. This makes
AI-replicated products appear like a Cambrian Explosion.

I love the fact that AI can make a proof-of-concept in a short
time. But I don't see the meaning of these projects. They are not
creative and not well-engineered. Very few will keep maintaining the
project. This slop gives people the impression that AI can fully
replace software engineers now (I think it indeed will replace SWEs
someday in the future). More importantly, it reduces the value of
creativity.

Seeing AI slop keeps consuming my cognitive energy. Still, AI can be
good and bad at the same time. Dear poor math and computer science
students, you will be fine, may the god bless you. I'm tired, yes, but
a quiet thrill stirs in me, the possibility of witnessing what might
emerge next.
